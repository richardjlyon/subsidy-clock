What is it?

When the cables cannot carry all the power from wind farms, most often from Scotland to England, the grid operator pays some wind farms to switch off. Those switch-off payments are counted on their own line. But homes and businesses south of the bottleneck still need the electricity the wind farms would have made, so the grid operator also pays other power stations, mostly gas, to switch on and make it instead. That second payment is the turn-up. The same unit of electricity is paid for twice: once to the wind farm not to make it, and once to a gas station to make it.

Why does it exist?

Wind farms were built faster than the transmission lines to carry their output. Until the network catches up, every unit switched off behind a bottleneck has to be replaced by a station on the other side of it.

What has it cost?

in cash as paid since 2015, currently growing at about a year — every second. Spread across Britain's households, that is about per household per year, paid through electricity bills.

Illustrative bill, not your tariff. The grid operator recovers turn-up through its balancing charge (BSUoS), which suppliers fold into the unit rate. This money used to sit inside the balancing line; it is now shown on its own, and the balancing line is smaller by the same amount, so the total does not change.

How sure are we?

This is an estimate, which is why it sits with the indirect costs rather than with the payments to renewable generators. It follows the method used by Octopus Energy's Wasted Wind tracker. For each half-hour, we take the amount of wind energy switched off for network reasons and price it at what the grid operator paid that half-hour to buy energy from other stations. We reproduce Octopus's published figures to within 0.1%: £1,466.8m for 2025, against Octopus's £1,467.0m. The amount of wind switched off is measured; what is estimated is its price. The grid operator's own figure for all its thermal-constraint costs is higher still, at £1,831m for 2025, because it covers more than replacing switched-off wind.

We also count what was actually paid, half-hour by half-hour, to stations other than wind farms, on offers the grid operator marked as constraint actions, while wind was being switched off. That came to since 2015, about of it to gas stations. It is lower than the estimate because the grid operator buys much of the replacement energy through ordinary trading, which is not marked; it is a floor, not a contradiction. Attribution confidence is low.
