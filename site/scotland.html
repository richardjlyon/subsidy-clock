How this is worked out

  • Payments are the bids accepted from each wind unit in the Balancing Mechanism to cut its output, unit by unit and half-hour by half-hour, from Elexon’s public settlement data. The same figures feed the switch-off card on the front page. This record starts on .
  • Which farms are Scottish comes from Elexon’s own register of units, not a hand-kept list. Elexon sets a transmission loss factor for each grid zone; North Scotland and South Scotland are two of the zones. Every unit paid in this period is placed; the full data lists them.
  • Who pays assumes the bill is shared in proportion to the electricity each nation uses. Switch-off costs are recovered through balancing charges, which suppliers pay for every unit of electricity their customers use. Consumption figures: .
  • What it leaves out: small wind farms connected to local networks rather than the national grid, which are not in the Balancing Mechanism; and the cost of the new lines being built to reduce constraints, which is charged separately.
